How to Verify a Bitcoin Address Before Sending BTC

A Bitcoin address is not a verified identity. Before sending, confirm that you have the recipient’s current full address, selected Bitcoin rather than another network, and that the wallet’s final review screen still shows the same destination. Matching only the first and last characters is not enough.[1]

Facts and practical guidance

Fact: Bitcoin.org warns that Bitcoin transactions are generally irreversible, malware may replace a copied address, and lookalike addresses can be used to mislead people copying from transaction history.[1]

Guidance: The checklist below reduces common mistakes; it cannot prove that a device is malware-free, authenticate a recipient, or guarantee recovery.

A pre-send verification routine

  1. Confirm the asset and network. Ask the recipient to specify BTC on the Bitcoin network. Do not infer compatibility from a token label or wallet icon. If a service requests another network, stop and check its official instructions.
  2. Get the destination through a trusted, independent channel. Confirm the current address with the recipient. Do not assume an address from a stranger’s message, an ad, or old transaction history is still appropriate.
  3. Compare the entire address after pasting or scanning. Check the full string displayed in your wallet. A QR code should still resolve to an address you verify. Bitcoin.org specifically cautions against checking only a few leading or trailing characters.[1]
  4. Watch for clipboard substitution and lookalikes. If the pasted address changes unexpectedly, or a saved/history address is similar but not identical, cancel and obtain it again independently. Address poisoning uses lookalike entries in transaction history.[1]
  5. Review the wallet’s final confirmation screen. Recheck the full destination, amount, asset/network, and displayed fee. Bitcoin Core’s send RPC takes the destination address and BTC amount as explicit inputs; a syntactically acceptable address does not authenticate who controls it.[3]
  6. Pause if anything changes. Verify sudden address changes, urgency, secrecy, or refund promises using a known second channel. Never disclose a recovery phrase or private key.[1]

Checklist

  • Did I verify the recipient independently?
  • Is this BTC on the intended Bitcoin network?
  • Does the full address match after entry and on the final review screen?
  • Am I avoiding reliance on transaction history, partial character matching, or an unverified QR code?
  • Have I checked the amount, fee, and recipient-service requirements?

If you independently decide to make a large transfer, a small test may be considered; it creates another transaction and fee, does not replace address verification, and does not prove the recipient’s identity.

Risk notice

This is general security education, not investment advice, a recommendation to buy or sell, or a promise of fund recovery. Blockchain transfers can be irreversible; no checklist eliminates device, recipient, or service risk. Never share a seed phrase, private key, or verification code.
