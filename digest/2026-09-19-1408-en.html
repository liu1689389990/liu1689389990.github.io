Sep 19, 2026 Crypto Close · Bitcoin trades around $81,000 as the CFTC takes its own pa

Bitcoin trades around $81,000 as the CFTC takes its own path on crypto rules, Coinbase files for single-stock perps, and Ethereum developers push to cut block times. Here's what moved crypto markets on Sept. 18.

01

CFTC starts its own crypto rulemaking after Congress stalls

The CFTC filed a prerule with the White House on Sept. 18, asking regulators to vet a framework for crypto asset transactions and markets. The filing comes a day after the Senate blocked the Clarity Act in a 49-50 vote, short of the 60 votes needed to advance.

Sen. Cynthia Lummis, the bill's lead negotiator, said chances of passing it this year are "all but dead." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has pointed to agency rulemaking as the fallback, leaving the CFTC move as the nearest thing to a regulatory timeline for U.S. crypto markets.

Why it matters

The CFTC is building a derivatives framework on its own authority rather than waiting for Congress — the infrastructure institutional money needs before it can trade crypto at scale.

02

Ethereum Institution backs plan to cut block times to 10 seconds

Ethereum Institutional, a non-profit, backed Ethlabs' motion to cut Ethereum block times from 12 seconds to 10 seconds, arguing faster blocks are needed as more institutional activity moves onchain. Ethlabs published an article Thursday quoting 20 DeFi founders backing EIP-8198, also known as "Quick Slots."

The push follows similar moves across the industry. Solana cut its slot time from 400ms to 350ms in August and plans to reach 200ms. Zcash holders backed cutting their target block time to 25 seconds from 75 seconds on Monday. EIP-8198 was authored in March and proposed for the Hegotá upgrade; Ethlabs is merging the specs into the main codebase, with developers possibly starting implementation in late 2026 after the Glamsterdam upgrade.

Why it matters

Block time is the interval between new blocks. Cutting 12 to 10 seconds shaves roughly 17% off the wait for the next block — meaningful when Ethereum is competing against chains that settle in well under a second.

03

Coinbase files to list single-stock perps on Apple, Tesla, Nvidia

Coinbase Derivatives filed with the CFTC on Sept. 18 for single-stock perpetual futures, letting U.S. traders hold leveraged positions in individual stocks without owning the shares. The WSJ reported Coinbase plans roughly 50 to 60 contracts at launch, covering Apple, Microsoft, Tesla and Nvidia.

Perpetual futures carry no expiration date — traders hold positions indefinitely as long as they meet margin requirements. They have been a crypto staple for years but were off-limits to U.S. traders in most forms until Coinbase became the first U.S. exchange cleared to offer regulated crypto perps earlier this year, later rolling out contracts with leverage up to 50x. The company already offers stock perps to non-U.S. traders, having launched the product in March.

What to watch: The CFTC prerule review by OIRA could take weeks to months before any text goes public. Coinbase's filing is approval-pending — no launch date is set. EIP-8198 still needs to clear the core developer process before landing in Hegotá.

Why it matters

Single-stock perps bring the high-leverage format that made crypto exchanges popular into the U.S. stock market. If approved, U.S. traders could take leveraged bets on Apple or Nvidia without options accounts.
