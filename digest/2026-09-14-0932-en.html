Sep 14, 2026 Crypto Close · Bitcoin price today (Sept. 14): BTC trades near $76,800, d

Bitcoin price today (Sept. 14): BTC trades near $76,800, down 0.6% in 24 hours, while ethereum sits at $2,485 and altcoins like Solana slipped 2.5%. Goldman Sachs now expects a Fed rate hike Wednesday. Here's the crypto news that matters this morning.

01

Goldman drops its hold call as a Fed rate hike looks near-certain

Goldman Sachs late Friday became the last major bank to retract its forecast of no rate hike, now expecting the Federal Reserve to raise rates by 25 basis points on Wednesday. Futures markets price in a nearly 90% chance of a hike, according to Goldman's note.

The debate is over why. Core CPI has fallen to a five-year low of 2.4%, and strategist James Thorne of Wellington-Altus said the move would be about calming Wall Street, not inflation. Economist Diane Swonk disagreed, arguing the inflation picture is worsening beyond what the core CPI report showed.

Why it matters

A hike lifts bond yields and drains liquidity from risk assets. Wednesday's fed rate decision is the biggest event of the week for bitcoin and ethereum prices.

02

Circle's $400M Tazapay deal buys emerging-market rails for USDC

Circle is acquiring Singapore-based Tazapay for a reported $400 million, adding payout rails in more than 100 markets and banking relationships that would take "years to build," payments chief Irfan Ganchi told CoinDesk.

USDC is the second-largest stablecoin with about $74 billion in circulation, behind Tether's USDT, in a market worth over $300 billion. Circle is also building its own blockchain, Arc, and the Circle Payments Network for cross-border transfers.

Why it matters

Stablecoin issuers are now fighting for emerging markets where Tether has long been strong. The last-mile connections decide where USDC can actually be spent.

03

Revolut attackers start leaking customer data, threaten daily releases

Attackers who obtained Revolut customer data have published identity documents and selfies, including files belonging to tennis player Alexander Shevchenko and Gamdom CEO Felix Römer, according to monitoring group International Cyber Digest. The attackers said they will release more data every day until "revolut pays."

Revolut said earlier that passports, verification selfies and transaction histories were exposed after it responded to a fraudulent request that appeared to come from a government agency email domain.

📌 This week: the Fed decides on Wednesday, and a 25 basis point hike is the base case. Expect choppy bitcoin price action around the announcement.

Why it matters

The breach shows phishing that mimics government agencies can pass corporate verification checks. Users of crypto exchanges and fintech apps should treat any sudden identity re-checks with caution.
