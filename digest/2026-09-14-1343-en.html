Bitcoin price today, Monday, Sept. 14: BTC trades around $77,500, up 0.3% over 24 hours, and ether holds near $2,510. CryptoQuant says bitcoin must clear $81,700 to confirm a new bull market. Here's the crypto news moving markets in Asian hours.

01

Bitcoin must clear $81,700 to confirm a new bull market, CryptoQuant says

Bitcoin's 24% rally over two weeks has stalled, with the price moving between $76,000 and $82,000 and trading around $77,000, according to a Friday report from onchain analytics firm CryptoQuant.

The heaviest resistance sits between $77,100 and $80,200, where long-term holders sold as much as 539,000 BTC over 30 days this year, said Julio Moreno, the firm's head of research. Above that zone, bitcoin's 365-day moving average near $81,700 is the first major technical level. Bull markets have historically been confirmed only when the price closes above that average.

Why it matters

Until bitcoin reclaims $81,700, the rally stays a range trade. A clean close above it is the signal traders will watch for a new bull market.

02

Symbiosis recovers 15 BTC after bitcoin bridge exploit, offers 20% bounty for the funds' return

Cross-chain protocol Symbiosis said it recovered about 15 BTC, worth $1.15 million at current prices, after an attacker exploited a vulnerability in its Bitcoin Bridge on Sept. 11.

The recovered bitcoin sits in a team-controlled multisig wallet. Symbiosis paused its native bitcoin routes but kept routes on Ethereum-compatible networks, TRON and TON running, and restored bitcoin swaps through Chainflip and THORChain.

The 20% white-hat bounty offered to the attacker closed Sept. 13. The same 20% reward now goes to anyone whose information leads to further recovery, and the project says it is building a compensation plan for affected liquidity providers.

Why it matters

Bridge exploits remain one of DeFi's biggest security risks, and public bounties are one of the few ways projects get stolen funds back.

03

Robinhood CEO says issuers should not get a veto over tokenized stocks

Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev said companies should not have veto power over tokenized stock products that leave shareholder rights, issuer obligations and the official stock ledger unchanged.

Products that merely reference freely transferable shares need no consent, Tenev said in a Friday post on X. "Going onchain shouldn't give the issuer a veto it never had offchain," he said.

AMC Entertainment CEO Adam Aron criticized Robinhood's tokenized stocks on Sept. 4, saying AMC had no affiliation with them. Bernstein estimates the blockchain push could generate $160 million in annual fees by 2028.

Why it matters

This dispute decides who controls tokenized equities as brokers and exchanges race to put stocks onchain.
