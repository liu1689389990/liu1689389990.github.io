Sep 15, 2026 Crypto Close · Bitcoin price today (Sept. 15): BTC trades around $77,400

Bitcoin price today (Sept. 15): BTC trades around $77,400 while Ethereum holds near $2,480. In this crypto news briefing: CoinEx shuts down after nine years, South Korea's crypto tax fight returns, and Balancer proposes a wind-down.

01

CoinEx to shut down after nine years as trading volumes sink

Crypto exchange CoinEx said Tuesday it is winding down operations, citing falling trading volumes, weak liquidity and rising compliance costs. Withdrawals stay open until Dec. 22.

Spot trading ends Sept. 29, and any USDT left after the deadline moves to an independent custodian that charges a monthly fee. The exchange will buy back its CET token at the initial listing price of 0.005 USDT. CoinEx launched in December 2017 from mining pool ViaBTC.

CEO Haipo Yang said the security and compliance risks of running an exchange had become too difficult to contain. CoinEx joins BitMart, BitMEX and AscendEX among exchanges that ceased operations this year.

Why it matters

Mid-tier exchanges are losing the liquidity war as regulation drives up operating costs. Fewer venues could mean thinner trading for smaller altcoins.

02

South Korean investors petition to delay the crypto tax again

A petition to postpone South Korea's crypto gains tax reached the 50,000-signature threshold, forcing a formal review by the National Assembly. The tax is set to take effect Jan. 1, 2027, at an effective rate of 22% on annual gains above 2.5 million won ($1,856).

The plan has already been delayed three times since 2022. Petitioners say most local investors are sitting on losses and the tax would push traders to offshore platforms. Finance minister nominee Lee Hyoung-Il said over the weekend that the tax remains on schedule.

Why it matters

South Korea is one of the most active retail crypto markets, and the standoff shows how hard it is for governments to tax volatile digital assets.

03

Balancer proposes wind-down and a treasury payout to BAL holders

Balancer, one of the oldest automated market makers on the Ethereum blockchain, proposed shutting down the protocol and distributing its remaining treasury, worth more than $9 million, to BAL tokenholders.

Balancer Labs CEO Marcus Hardt published the proposal on the governance forum Monday. He said the v3 upgrade failed to replace the revenue lost after a $128 million exploit in November, even though the team cut costs and shipped new products.

Why it matters

Even long-established DeFi protocols struggle to recover from a major hack. An orderly treasury payout is a rare outcome for tokenholders.
